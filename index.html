Beavers Are Having a Moment

Reasons to be Cheerful · 14 hours ago

In parts of Oregon that had been hit hard by drought, wildfires, and vanishing salmon, people are turning to an old ally for help: beavers. Their dams naturally slow and spread out water, recharge groundwater, and create lush wetlands that resist fire and support wildlife. After years of small, scattered fixes, communities are now actively welcoming beavers back and even helping them build in key spots. The result is real, measurable healing of landscapes that had been struggling for decades. It's a wonderful reminder that nature often already has the tools we need.

Firm Buys Texas Coal Plant to Leverage its Grid Connections for New Massive Solar Project

Good News Network · 5 hours ago

An energy investment firm just bought a coal plant in Texas, not to keep burning coal, but to use its existing power lines and grid connections for a brand new solar and battery storage project. The $1.7 billion plan, called Big Rooter Power, will turn old fossil fuel infrastructure into clean energy capacity in Robertson, Texas. It is a clever shortcut: instead of building new transmission lines from scratch, which can take years, they are reusing what is already there. This kind of practical repurposing could speed up the shift to renewables across the country.
