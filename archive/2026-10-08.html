NYC Parks Extends Late Night Soccer at 5 Fields for Night Workers–And Their Reactions Are Awesome

Good News Network · 7 hours ago

New York City decided to keep the lights on at five soccer fields across all five boroughs so that people who work overnight shifts can finally get a chance to play. It's a small change with a big impact: thousands of nurses, delivery drivers, and other night-shift workers no longer have to choose between a paycheck and a pickup game. So far more than 4,500 people have shown up to enjoy the fields. It's a simple, thoughtful fix that makes city life a little fairer for people whose schedules don't match the usual nine-to-five. Watching their genuine excitement in the video is a nice reminder that good policy doesn't have to be complicated.

How NYC Students Saved School Lunch

Reasons to be Cheerful · 9 hours ago

A group of New York City students decided they were tired of skipping the cafeteria because the food was bad and eating there felt embarrassing. Instead of just complaining, they worked with their school to redesign the lunch program itself, improving the food and the whole experience of eating together. More students are now actually showing up to eat real meals instead of avoiding the cafeteria. It's a simple, human win: kids got a say in something that affects them every single day, and it worked.

More Than 14,000 Derelict Explosives Cleared from Libyan City and Nearby Farmland

Good News Network · 1 hour ago

A coastal city in Libya and the farmland around it are a lot safer this year, thanks to a careful team effort. The United Nations' mission in Libya, Italian aid workers, local Libyan partners, and the demining group HALO Trust worked together to clear more than 14,000 old landmines and unexploded bombs left over from past conflict. That means farmers can till their fields without fear, and families can walk their own streets without worrying about what's buried beneath them. It's slow, careful work, but it's already done and it's already making daily life safer for real people.
