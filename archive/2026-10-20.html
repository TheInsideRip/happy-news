Volunteers became salmon, deer and earthworms. Then they set out to save their river

Positive News · 39 hours ago

In Somerset, England, a group of local volunteers took part in a creative exercise where they imagined themselves as salmon, deer, and even earthworms living along their local river. The idea was to help people see the landscape through the eyes of the wildlife that depends on it. From that exercise, the community built a real 30-year plan to restore the health of the river and its surrounding land. It's a lovely example of neighbors coming together, using imagination and patience, to care for the nature right outside their doors.

Isle of Jersey Expands Marine Protections to Cover 21% of Territorial Waters

Good News Network · 2 hours ago

The island of Jersey just made a big move to protect its seas. Officials expanded the area of ocean shielded from trawling and other damaging fishing from about 7% to nearly 22% of their waters. The new rules took effect on September 1st, giving fish, coral, and seafloor habitats room to recover and thrive. It's a real, binding change, not just a promise, and it shows how even a small island can make an outsized difference for ocean health.

The New Certification Scheme Helping Solar Farms Become Havens for Wildlife

Positive News · 4 hours ago

A new certification scheme has launched to reward solar farm developers who design their sites to also support local wildlife. Instead of just rows of panels on bare ground, certified farms now include wildflower meadows, hedgerows, and ponds that give bees, birds, and small animals a place to thrive. It's a simple idea that turns clean energy sites into little nature reserves at the same time. Developers are already signing up, meaning more solar farms across the country will soon be doing double duty for the planet.
