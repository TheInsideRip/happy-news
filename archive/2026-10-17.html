99-Year-Old Becomes World's Oldest Wing-Walker

Good News Network · 3 hours ago

A 99-year-old woman named Helga Duffy just earned the Guinness World Record for being the oldest person ever to go wing-walking, strapped to the top of a biplane as it soared through the sky. She was inspired after her niece told her about another elderly woman who'd tried it, and the staff at her assisted-living home encouraged her to go for it herself. Guinness officially confirmed her record this week, making her living proof that it's never too late to try something thrilling and new. It's a joyful reminder that age doesn't have to limit a sense of adventure.

Coral Bleaching Reversed at Record Speed Using New Stanford Method

Good News Network · just now

Scientists at Stanford found a way to help bleached, ghostly-white coral turn back to full, healthy color in just seven weeks in their lab. That's the fastest recovery from bleaching ever recorded by researchers. Coral bleaching happens when warming waters stress reefs and they lose the colorful algae that keep them alive, so finding a way to speed up their comeback is a big deal. The team says this proves coral reefs are 'not a lost cause, at all,' giving real hope for the oceans. It's a hands-on breakthrough, not just a theory, and it could help guide real reef rescues in the future.

Teen's Disaster Messaging App Can 'Hop' Across Bluetooth When Cellular Networks Collapse

Good News Network · 22 hours ago

A teenager built an app that lets phones talk to each other over Bluetooth, hopping from device to device, even when cell towers are knocked out. The idea came from the 2023 earthquakes in Turkey and Syria, where collapsed networks left people unable to call for help when they needed it most. Because it doesn't rely on cell service or the internet, the app could let neighbors relay emergency messages during hurricanes, earthquakes, or any disaster that takes down the grid. It's a clever, low-cost fix built by someone young enough to have grown up watching these disasters unfold on the news. Simple ideas like this one could genuinely save lives in the next crisis.
